# Drafts trained on your traffic

Speculative decoding is fast only when the draft guesses what your model will write. A draft trained
on public chat guesses public chat; on your traffic (your languages, your system prompts, your
formats) it guesses less often. A draft trained on prompts from your traffic guesses more often. The
training runs in your account, and a campaign measures the result against what you serve today before
anything changes.

This page covers three things:

- where the training data comes from, and what is stored where;
- the training job that runs in your cluster;
- how a trained draft becomes a setup a campaign measures.

## What is built and what is not

| Part | State |

| The trained-draft technique in the [technique contract](/features/technique-contract) (`speculator.Technique`, `trained-draft` 0.1.0) | Built, tested |

Drafts in speculators' own format carry their embedding and are not affected |

Conversion is judged by served acceptance: the shipped and converted drafts both serve at 1.66 |
| A measured gain on one small model (rung 2) | **One machine, not a shown claim.** `Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B` in BF16 on one L4, vLLM 0.34.0, 128 held-out oasst1 chat prompts (thinking off, answers up to 256 tokens). Answers showed no sign of change by identical greedy text, which no speculation itself repeats for about a quarter of prompts, so quality is **not shown**. ## How it works

1. **Prompts stay in your account.** Either you give the job a file of prompts, or the endpoint
   captures prompts for you after you turn capture on. Neither leaves your cluster or bucket.
2. **The job trains in your cluster.** A Kubernetes Job runs a pinned trainer image on your GPU. Your
   model answers every prompt at temperature 0 inside the job, and the draft learns from those
   answers. It starts from a published draft for the same model when there is one.
3. **Numbers come back.** The job writes the draft and a report beside it. The report holds counts,
   digests and acceptance shares, never text.
4. **You name the draft in a plan.** With `diagnosis_endpoint` and `speculative_drafts`, the planner
   adds a setup that serves your draft. It also adds the setups your draft must beat.
5. **The judge decides.** The campaign measures each setup against your current one on your traffic
   shape, scores answers on a task set, and confirms a gain on fresh servers. Nothing is applied
   until you approve.

## Where the training data comes from

The draft learns from **your model's answers**, not your users' answers: EAGLE-3 drafts imitate the
model they serve, so the job has the model answer each prompt again. Only prompts are needed.

### A prompt file you choose (built)

Put a JSON-lines file on a volume in your cluster, one conversation per line:

```json
{"id": "support-0001", "messages": [{"role": "system", "content": "You are Acme's support agent."}, {"role": "user", "content": "My invoice shows two charges."}]}
```

Use prompts you are allowed to process this way: a sample you already keep, synthetic prompts in your
format, or a public dataset of the same kind. The job reads the file read-only. The job splits off
10% as held-out prompts it never trains on and scores the draft on them.

### Capture from an endpoint (designed, not built)

Capture is off by default. It is turned on per endpoint by an operator, and only on an endpoint whose
traffic is served by an InferCrane agent in your cluster. The hosted gateway never captures.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **What is stored** | The request's `messages` (or `prompt`) and the model name, under a random record id, with the time it was received. Never answers, headers, API keys, key ids, user ids or addresses. Requests with images, audio or tool results are skipped. |
| **Which requests** | A sample: 1% by default, set per endpoint, at most 50,000 records or 2 GiB per capture, whichever comes first. |
| **Where** | A PersistentVolumeClaim or a bucket in your account that the operator names. The agent's data plane writes it; the control plane has no access. |
| **What the control plane sees** | Capture on or off, record count, bytes, oldest record's age, and a digest of the record ids. No text. |
| **How long** | 14 days by default, at most 30. The agent deletes expired records every hour. |
| **How it is deleted** | Turning capture off deletes every record of that endpoint within an hour. `infercrane draft capture delete ENDPOINT` deletes at once. A training job deletes its working copy of prompts and generated answers when it ends. The report and the draft remain. |
| **Who can read it** | The cluster's operator and the training job, which mounts it read-only. |

<Warning>
A draft trained on your prompts is derived from them and can reproduce phrases it saw often. Keep it
where you keep the prompts: a private Hugging Face repository in your organisation, or your bucket.
Delete it when you delete the data.
</Warning>

Capture is not built yet. It needs a change to the agent's data plane, so the invariant "prompts,
completions, and request content never leave an agent's machine or cluster" holds exactly as
written: captured prompts stay on the operator's volume, and nothing new goes to the control plane
except the counts above.

## The training job

`speculator.TrainingJob` is a bounded spec, not a shell command. `KubernetesJob()` renders it as a
`batch/v1` Job:

- the trainer image, pinned by digest, which the operator allows;
- the prompt volume mounted read-only at `/data` and an output volume at `/output`;
- no service account token, a non-root user and no added capabilities;
- `backoffLimit: 0`, so a failed run never trains twice;
- the job's own deadline, and deletion an hour after it ends;
- `HF_TOKEN` only from a Kubernetes Secret you name, for a gated model or a private starting draft.

```json
{
  "name": "support-chat",
  "base_model": "Qwen/Qwen3-8B",
  "base_revision": "b968826d9c46dd6066d109eabc6255188de91218",
  "start_from": {"repository": "RedHatAI/Qwen3-8B-speculator.eagle3", "revision": "08610ffa01dd9f16731fe8f627b85905b6aa51c4"},
  "data_source": "prompt_set",
  "data_path": "prompts/support.jsonl",
  "image": "registry.example.com/infercrane/draft-trainer@sha256:...",
  "accelerator": "H100",
  "namespace": "inference",
  "data_claim": "prompts",
  "output_claim": "drafts"
}
```

Defaults: method `eagle3`, 10% held out, 50,000 prompts, 20 million answer tokens, 1,024 tokens per
answer, 3 draft tokens, 1 epoch, 1 GPU, 6 hours. Upper bounds: 200,000 prompts, 200 million tokens,
8 GPUs, 24 hours.

The trainer is [speculators](https://github.com/vllm-project/speculators) 0.9.0 (Apache-2.0). vLLM
0.34.0 serves its output format directly, and it can start from a published EAGLE-3 draft. While
training, a vLLM server in the same job computes the model's hidden states on demand
(`extract_hidden_states`). Nothing is stored between steps.

### The report

The job writes `report.json` beside the draft (`speculator.ReportSchema`). `DecodeReport` refuses
unknown fields, so a report cannot carry text:

```json
{
  "schema": "infercrane.dev/draft-training-report/v1",
  "name": "support-chat", "method": "eagle3", "draft_tokens": 3,
  "base_model": "Qwen/Qwen3-8B", "base_revision": "b968826d9c46dd6066d109eabc6255188de91218",
  "start_from": {"repository": "RedHatAI/Qwen3-8B-speculator.eagle3", "revision": "08610ffa01dd9f16731fe8f627b85905b6aa51c4"},
  "framework": "speculators", "framework_version": "0.9.0", "data_source": "prompt_set",
  "dataset_fingerprint": "sha256:...", "examples": 9000, "held_out_examples": 1000, "training_tokens": 4100000,
  "held_out_acceptance": [0.81, 0.66, 0.55], "start_acceptance": [0.74, 0.58, 0.47],
  "produced_at": "2026-11-26T09:00:00Z",
  "files": [{"path": "config.json", "size": 1290, "sha256": "..."}, {"path": "model.safetensors", "size": 1610612736, "sha256": "..."}],
  "artifact_digest": "sha256:...", "wall_seconds": 2400
}
```

- `dataset_fingerprint` is the sha256 of the sorted example digests. It tells two training sets apart
  without holding either.
- `held_out_acceptance` gives, per draft position, the share of held-out positions where the draft's
  token was the model's own, given the earlier positions were. It is an **offline estimate**. With the
  values above, a step writes about 1 + 0.81 + 0.81 × 0.66 + 0.81 × 0.66 × 0.55 ≈ 2.6 tokens. Only a
  campaign says whether serving gets faster.
- `artifact_digest` is the sha256 of the file list: `path size sha256`, one line per file, sorted by
  path. A draft kept in a bucket is pinned by this digest.

## Name the draft in a plan

Push the draft to a private Hugging Face repository in your organisation and pin its commit. Then ask
for a plan from the endpoint's diagnosis:

```json
{
  "model_identity": "Qwen/Qwen3-8B",
  "model_revision": "b968826d9c46dd6066d109eabc6255188de91218",
  "provider": "kubernetes", "gpu": "H100",
  "diagnosis_endpoint": "support-chat",
  "model_secret_reference_id": "hf-token",
  "speculative_drafts": [{
    "name": "support-chat", "source": "trained", "method": "eagle3",
    "repository": "acme/qwen3-8b-support-draft", "revision": "0123456789abcdef0123456789abcdef01234567",
    "base_model": "Qwen/Qwen3-8B", "base_revision": "b968826d9c46dd6066d109eabc6255188de91218",
    "draft_tokens": 3, "training": { "...": "the report's provenance fields" }
  }]
}
```

- At most one trained draft and one published draft. Each must be trained against exactly the plan's
  model and commit: a draft reads that model's hidden layers.
- Drafts need `diagnosis_endpoint`. The records enter the proposal's input and its digest; the files
  never do.
- A private draft is downloaded by the server with the same `model_secret_reference_id` as the model.

### What the plan measures

Rule `drafts.supplied` (rule set `diagnosis-technique-rules-v11`) adds these setups:

| Setup | Order |
|---|---|
| **Your trained draft** (`trained-draft`; earlier `speculative-trained-draft`) | Right after the strongest speculation choice of the other rules. When no rule chose speculation, after the changes the diagnosis pointed at. |
| The published draft (`speculative-draft-model`) | Two places below your draft. |
| The model's own prediction head (`speculative-mtp`) | Four places below, only when the model's config declares one. |
| No speculation (`speculation-off`) | Six places below, only when your current setup speculates. When it does not, your current setup is the no-speculation arm. |

A setup another rule already chose keeps that rule's place. Each draft setup removes whatever
speculation your current setup had before it adds the draft:

```text
--speculative-config {"method":"eagle3","model":"acme/qwen3-8b-support-draft","revision":"0123…","num_speculative_tokens":3}
```

A plan skips a draft, and says why, in these cases:

- the server runs SGLang or a pinned custom image (only vLLM flags are reviewed);
- the draft is in a bucket (not served yet);
- the model is 1B parameters or smaller;
- the draft record names no pin, or a pin to another engine version or GPU (see below).

Speculation can change what the model writes, so every draft setup needs a task score against your
current setup ([how a campaign checks answers](/features/optimization#how-a-campaign-checks-answers)).

## Use it without InferCrane's control plane

Every step runs from the CLI on your own machine, against any vLLM server, on any provider. Nothing is
sent anywhere.

```bash
infercrane optimize draft job --file job.json | kubectl apply -f -
```

```bash
infercrane optimize draft record --report report.json --repository acme/qwen3-1.7b-chat-draft --revision COMMIT --engine vllm --engine-version 0.34.0 --accelerator L4 > draft.json
```

```bash
vllm serve Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B $(infercrane optimize draft args --record draft.json)
```

```bash
infercrane optimize draft candidates --record draft.json --setup setup.json
```

`candidates` asks the trained-draft technique for its candidates in the
[technique contract](/features/technique-contract), the same answer the optimizer gets. A candidate
names the draft by its artifact digest, carries the starting setup's flags with the draft's
speculation in place of its own, and predicts a low-load speed range from the offline acceptance
in the report. The judge, not the prediction, decides.

## Pinned, with a fallback

A draft record is pinned to the engine version and GPU where it was built and qualified (`pinned`).
On another engine version or GPU the technique proposes nothing and the starting setup serves
unchanged, until the draft is qualified there. The same holds when the draft's files are missing: a
setup without its draft is the starting setup. The training job maps onto the artifact store's
provenance (`optimizedartifact.Plan`: tool `vllm-speculators`, exact version, builder image digest, the
dataset fingerprint as the calibration digest, quality review required), and a finished report maps onto
its attestation (repository, immutable revision, manifest digest).

The draft is not built through `acceleratorlab`. That lab runs builds in InferCrane's own sandboxes, and a
build that reads your prompts runs in your account (the technique contract refuses anything else).

## What a result can and cannot say

- The campaign compares each setup with your current one on the traffic shape you replay. With a prompt
  corpus, the text is real; without one, acceptance on your content stays unknown (see
  [how a campaign measures candidates](/features/optimization#how-a-campaign-measures-candidates)).
- A draft is trained on traffic up to a date. When your traffic changes, its acceptance falls. Check
  `produced_at`, and train again when a stay-optimized check shows acceptance dropping.
- Every trained-draft result carries the evidence the technique's manifest requires,
  `draft-acceptance`: acceptance read from vLLM's own speculation counters over the measured runs,
  in a file whose sha256 the [technique record](/features/technique-contract) holds. A record
  without it, or from a harness commit with uncommitted changes, is "not shown". Like every
  technique's result, it is also "not shown" until a fresh machine has measured the gain again
  (`fresh-machine-confirmation`).
- Company figures for this technique are company-reported. Together AI reports 1.23x to 1.45x over its
  base speculator; Fireworks AI reports a 76% against 29% hit rate. They are not InferCrane
  measurements, and no InferCrane page compares with them as if they were.
